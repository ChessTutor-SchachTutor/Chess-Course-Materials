## The idea behind the handbook

I developed and wrote this handbook in Germany, originally in German, and translated it into English with AI assistance to make it accessible to more people.

The handbook connects scientific findings on chess with my experience as a chess coach and schoolteacher. My central hypothesis is that purposefully used strategy games can stimulate thinking processes similar to those involved in chess instruction. Observations from my courses provide initial indications in support of this approach.

These games allow children to practise individual aspects of chess thinking without first having to acquire extensive chess knowledge. That is why I consider this approach so valuable. In my experience, many children stop playing after attending a school chess club before they have learned to reflect consciously and systematically on their decisions on the board. These simplified strategy games address that challenge: children can explore decisions and their consequences at a level they can understand.

## What the handbook contains

An extensive theoretical section explains the thinking behind my approach. Among other things, I compare chess as a school subject in Armenia with school chess clubs in Germany, examining how their contexts and objectives differ from one another and from simply introducing children to chess.

This is followed by the practical implementation: a selection and detailed analysis of over 20 strategy games that I consider useful for this purpose.

The final section summarises my experience from four courses at the Hector Children's Academy in Ulm, Germany. This enrichment programme is intended for particularly gifted primary school children, who do not necessarily have previous chess experience. I have run the programme there several times and developed it further through my observations. The full version also includes detailed course documentation.

## Developing chess thinking – also as a foundation for later chess training

The programme aims to develop thinking processes such as anticipating consequences, systematically checking possibilities and weighing alternatives. It is therefore particularly suitable for children who attend chess primarily for the hoped-for benefits to their thinking. In my experience, this applies to a large proportion of children attending school chess clubs. These strategy games pursue that goal through play: children face challenges and practise thinking consciously about their decisions.

**The main focus is on children for whom developing their thinking is the priority. In my experience, the approach can also provide a useful foundation for children who want to learn chess later.**

Rather than first getting to know as much of chess as possible, children use manageable games to practise approaching a position systematically, examining possibilities and explaining their decisions. This reflects the understanding of chess education that I associate with the Soviet chess school and that also underpins the rest of my chess course.

I have also used this approach successfully in chess coaching. For example, I helped a beginner reach 1700 Elo within two years, with one hour of tuition with me per week and less than an hour of independent training per day. This is an individual example from my coaching practice that, for me, illustrates the possibilities of this approach.

For me, this route is therefore no detour on the way to chess. It provides opportunities to practise ways of thinking that also matter in later chess training.

Those who want to learn chess afterwards can build on it. Those who stop have still practised thinking at an appropriate level – and that is exactly where I see the value of this programme.

## Why is it free?

Developing, writing and checking this handbook took me six full months of work. I want to make that work available to you to support your children's development.

**It is deliberately free for everyone. But please do not mistake free for worthless.** A great deal of work has gone into it, along with my conviction that this approach can give children something valuable.
